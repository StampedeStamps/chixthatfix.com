Why the name works

Say it once and it sticks. Chicks That Fix rhymes, says what the work is, and carries an attitude: capable, unbothered, a little cheeky. Three words, and no tagline needed.

It works as a show title, a shop sign, a hashtag, a tool line or a podcast name without a word of explanation. Women in the trades, women-run repair shops and women-led DIY channels are no longer a niche story, and the name that leads that story should be short, memorable and unmistakably theirs.

Both spellings come with the sale. ChicksThatFix.com is the full name. ChixThatFix.com is the short one: the spelling people type on a phone, and the one that fits a social handle. Owning both keeps imitators off the short spelling, and each address points wherever you choose.

Both spellings end in X, and the X is the logo: two crossed wrenches that read on the side of a van and in a browser tab alike.

How a sale works

  1. Email an offer.

    Include the figure you have in mind and, if you like, what you plan to build. You will hear back from the owner, not a broker.

  2. Agree the price in writing.

    No broker and no commission: the figure we agree is the figure you pay, plus the Escrow.com fee as we agree it.

  3. Pay through Escrow.com.

    Your funds are held by a licensed third party and released only when both domains are in your account.

  4. Take ownership.

    The transfer starts as soon as Escrow.com confirms your payment: an account change if you are at GoDaddy, or an authorization code for any other registrar.
